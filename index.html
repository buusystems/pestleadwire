How it works

From a health inspection to your Monday call list

  1. 01

    We read the records

    Every 6 hours we pull every public inspection feed we cover: 29 city, county and state feeds across 13 states.

  2. 02

    We keep the pest findings

    Rodents, droppings, roaches, flies, gaps where pests get in, missing pest control logs. Each one is scored for urgency. Businesses that already passed a re-inspection are left out.

  3. 03

    We split them by territory

    You give us your ZIP and how far you drive. Each business goes to one pest control company a week, nearest first, and never twice to the same company.

  4. 04

    You get the list Monday

    By email at 9 AM your time: a PDF you can tap to call or map, and an Excel file with the inspector's full notes.

The record What we find

  • Restaurants, groceries, bakeries, cafeterias and other food businesses cited on a routine or complaint inspection.
  • What the inspector wrote: "evidence of mice in basement," "live roaches at dish station," "no pest control log on site."

Your list What you get

  • Business name, address, phone (from the inspection feed or the business's Google listing), miles from you.
  • Facility type, inspection date, result, and what the inspector found.
  • Only yours: we don't send the same business to two companies in the same week.

Questions

Good to know

Where does the data come from?

Public health inspection records that cities, counties and states publish themselves, such as the City of Chicago, New York City, the State of Florida and King County. We don't scrape anything private.

Does a citation mean the business wants pest control?

No. It means an inspector noted a pest problem on that date. It doesn't mean they've asked for help, and they may already have a provider. It does mean they have a reason to take your call this week.

Will other pest control companies get the same leads?

Each business goes to one company a week. If you choose to accept shared leads to fill out a slow week, those are marked on your list.

Where do the phone numbers come from?

Some inspection feeds include them (New York City, Florida, Cincinnati, Raleigh). For the rest we look up the business's public Google listing. If we can't find one, the phone column is left blank. We never guess.

What if my area isn't covered?

Send us your ZIP anyway. We add places as their health departments publish data, and we'll tell you when yours goes live.
