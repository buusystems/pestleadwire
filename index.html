    What a pro here receives

    Records come straight from city open-data portals: NYC Department of Health restaurant inspections, NYC HPD housing violations and NYC 311; Chicago Department of Public Health food inspections and Chicago 311. A citation records what an inspector saw on that date; it does not mean the problem is still there. Housing and 311 locations are shown to the block, never the apartment.

    How it works

    From a city record to a booked job in four steps

    1. 01

      Signal

      We pull every new pest citation, infestation order and rodent complaint from city data as soon as it's published, and tag it by pest, zip and deadline.

    2. 02

      Score

      Each signal gets an urgency score. A restaurant facing a reinspection or a landlord with an open Class C order ranks above a single sighting on the sidewalk.

    3. 03

      Wire

      We send the lead to licensed pros who cover that zip, treat that pest, and have open capacity this week. Each pro sets their own zips and limits.

    4. 04

      Close

      The pro reaches the owner with the record in hand and a fix before the reinspection. The owner keeps the treatment report on file for the inspector.

    Demand Who needs a pro

    • Restaurants and food shops cited for mice, rats, roaches or flies, with a reinspection coming.
    • Landlords and managers ordered by the city to clear an infestation in a unit or common area.
    • Blocks with rising 311 complaints, where building owners want to get ahead of a citation.

    Supply Who fixes it

    • Licensed pest control operators, from one-truck shops to regional firms.
    • Matched by zip, pest and job type: commercial kitchens, multifamily, exclusion work.
    • Paced by capacity, so a pro only gets the leads they can answer this week.

    For pest control pros

    Leads that come with a reason to call

    Most lead lists are guesses. Ours are public records: the business, the address, the pest the inspector found and the date. You call an owner who already knows they have a problem and a deadline.

    • Choose your zips, your pests, and how many leads a week you want.
    • See the citation behind every lead before you pay for it.
    • One pro per lead in each zip, so you aren't racing five others to the door.
